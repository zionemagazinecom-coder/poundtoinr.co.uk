# IFSC Code for UK to India Money Transfers

## What an IFSC is

An IFSC is an eleven-character code that identifies an Indian bank branch participating in electronic payment systems. A UK-to-India transfer provider may ask for it when arranging an INR deposit into a recipient's Indian account.

The code helps route the local payout after pounds have been funded and converted. It does not identify the recipient by itself and should be used with the correct account name and number.

The <a href="https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/Notification/PDFs/CE244NE121012.pdf" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Reserve Bank of India material</a> confirms the role of IFSC in electronic funds transfer transactions. Requirements for a specific international provider can still vary.

## The eleven characters

An IFSC normally contains four letters identifying the bank, a zero in the fifth position and six characters identifying the branch. It is commonly displayed without spaces.

Do not infer a code from the bank's name or town. Two branches of the same bank can have different codes, and a branch can change after a merger or system migration.

Treat every character as important. The letter O and number zero, or the letter I and number one, can be confused when details are read from an image.

## Why providers ask

Many online transfer services collect GBP in the UK and arrange a local INR payout through an Indian banking partner. The IFSC helps that payout reach the intended branch or routing destination.

The sender may never use Indian NEFT, RTGS or IMPS directly. The provider handles the payout route, but it still needs valid local bank information.

An IFSC does not guarantee acceptance. Account status, recipient name, purpose, limits and compliance checks can also affect delivery.

## Where to find it

Ask the recipient to copy the IFSC from current internet banking, a recent bank statement, the bank's official app or an official branch source.

Older cheque books and saved messages can contain outdated details. Use a current source, especially if the bank merged, the branch moved or the account was migrated.

Avoid random code directories unless the result is confirmed against the bank's official information. A directory can be stale even when its format looks correct.

## Verify with the recipient

Send the code back through a trusted channel and ask the recipient to confirm the bank, branch and account it belongs to. Read it in groups to reduce mistakes.

Do not accept a last-minute account change from an unfamiliar phone number or compromised email. Contact the recipient through a known number before editing saved bank details.

For a first or important payment, a small test transfer can reduce operational risk, but it adds cost and does not replace identity checks.

## Check the account number

A valid IFSC cannot repair an incorrect account number. The account number identifies the recipient account while the IFSC supports routing.

Copy the account number rather than typing it from memory. Compare the first and last digits, total length and any leading zeros.

Some provider screens remove spaces automatically. Ensure that formatting changes did not remove a genuine leading zero or include punctuation from copied text.

## Match the account name

Enter the recipient's name as held by the bank. Initials, surname order and business names should follow the provider's instructions.

Name checking for an Indian payout may not work like UK Confirmation of Payee. Do not assume the screen has validated ownership merely because it accepts the details.

Ask the recipient for a current account-holder name, then review it alongside the account number and IFSC before paying.

## NRE and NRO accounts

An NRE or NRO account can use an IFSC like another Indian bank account, but the account type and permitted purpose matter.

Confirm whether the recipient wants funds in an NRE, NRO or ordinary resident account. A transfer sent to the wrong account type can create banking or record problems.

## Bank mergers

Indian bank mergers can lead to changes in branch details. A recipient may keep using an old code from a cheque or previous transfer even after their bank publishes a replacement.

Ask them to verify through current online banking. If the provider warns that the code is invalid or the bank name does not match, stop and recheck.

Do not substitute another branch code simply because it belongs to the same bank. The account may not be routed correctly.

## Saved recipients

Saved beneficiaries make repeat transfers easier, but their details can become stale. Review the account and IFSC periodically and whenever the recipient reports a bank change.

A provider may preserve an old recipient profile even after a branch migration. Edit only after independent confirmation.

Check the final review page instead of assuming the saved recipient remains accurate.

## International and local codes

IFSC is not the same as a SWIFT/BIC. IFSC supports Indian domestic routing, while SWIFT/BIC identifies institutions in international messaging.

A direct bank wire may request SWIFT information, bank address and correspondent details. A specialist INR payout may request only the local account information it needs.

Follow the exact fields shown by the chosen service. Never paste an IFSC into a SWIFT field or the reverse.

## Purpose information

Transfer services can ask why money is being sent. Choose the accurate purpose, such as family support, savings, education or a supported business payment.

The purpose is separate from the IFSC, but an inaccurate selection can still cause questions, rejection or record issues.

Keep an invoice or supporting explanation when paying a business or institution. Do not select a personal purpose merely to make the form shorter.

## Before authorising

Review the recipient name, account number, IFSC, bank, amount, purpose, fee, exchange rate and final INR payout on one screen.

Save the quote and recipient confirmation. A clear record helps if the payment is delayed or returned.

## Invalid code warnings

If the form rejects the IFSC, check for spaces, swapped characters and old branch details. Do not keep submitting guessed variations.

Ask the recipient to retrieve the code again from official banking. Contact the transfer provider if a verified current code is not accepted.

A warning can also mean the provider does not support that bank, branch, account type or payout route.

## Transfer delays

Incorrect or inconsistent recipient information can lead to manual review, payout rejection or return. The provider's activity page should show whether action is required.

The <a href="/guides/uk-to-india-money-transfer-time/">transfer time guide</a> explains funding, conversion and bank-processing stages. The <a href="/guides/track-money-transfer-uk-to-india/">tracking guide</a> covers references and escalation.

Do not immediately send a duplicate. Establish the first payment's status before creating another order.

## Returned money

When the Indian bank rejects a payout, the provider may need time to receive and process the return. Fees or reconversion can affect the GBP amount returned.

Ask for the rejection reason and confirm corrected details before trying again. Keep the original and return references.

There is no guarantee that every incorrect payment will automatically return, so report errors as soon as they are noticed.

## Security checks

Use the official provider website or app. Verify its regulatory identity and avoid links received through unsolicited messages.

Never share online-banking passwords, card PINs or one-time codes with a recipient or support agent. These are not required to receive rupees.

## Documents

The sender can be asked for identification, address evidence, source of funds or payment-purpose documents. The recipient may need to clarify account details.

These requests do not mean the IFSC is wrong. They are separate compliance and operational checks.

Prepare clear evidence using the <a href="/guides/documents-to-send-money-uk-to-india/">transfer documents guide</a> and submit it only through secure channels.

## Final checklist

Obtain the eleven-character IFSC from current official banking information. Confirm it with the recipient along with the account name and number.

Check the account type, bank, purpose and payout amount. Review every character on the final screen.

Keep the receipt and transfer reference. If delivery is delayed, ask the provider which detail or payout reference needs attention.

## Common questions

**Do I need an IFSC for every UK-to-India transfer?**

It is commonly required for INR bank deposits, but the fields depend on the provider and payout method.

**Is IFSC the same as SWIFT?**

No. IFSC supports Indian domestic routing; SWIFT/BIC is used in international bank messaging.

**Can I find an IFSC on a cheque?**

Often yes, but verify it against current official banking information because branch details can change.

**What happens if the IFSC is wrong?**

The payout may be rejected, delayed, returned or require investigation. Contact the provider immediately.

**Does a valid IFSC prove the account belongs to the recipient?**

No. Confirm the account name and number separately before authorising payment.
