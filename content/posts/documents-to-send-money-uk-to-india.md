# Documents Needed to Send Money from UK to India

## Start with the request

The **documents needed to send money from UK to India** depend on your bank or payment provider, your account verification status and the payment itself. Expect questions about identity, the source of the money and the intended recipient. Do not assume every customer must submit the same bundle of papers.

A routine transfer from an established account may require little new information. A first payment, changed personal details or a transaction that needs additional review can require more evidence.

The most useful preparation is to gather accurate records and follow the request inside the provider's official service. This guide offers a practical checklist, not legal advice or a universal approval guarantee.

## Why checks happen

UK customer due diligence rules require regulated businesses to identify and verify customers in relevant circumstances. HMRC guidance distinguishes the checks a firm must complete from the practical timing of a transaction.

Providers can also need to understand where money came from and whether a proposed payment fits the information they hold. A request for evidence does not automatically mean that you have done anything wrong.

Avoid assuming that a previously approved payment removes all future checks. New information or a different transaction can change the questions asked.

Keep explanations simple and truthful. If you are unsure what a request means, ask the provider which fact it needs you to demonstrate before uploading unrelated documents.

## Identity evidence

An identity check may require a valid passport or another supported government-issued photo document. Accepted documents vary by provider, residence and the issuing country.

Wise, for example, publishes lists covering passports, certain national identity cards and driving licences, with exceptions. That is a provider-specific policy rather than a promise that every UK-to-India service accepts the same documents.

Check that your account uses your legal name and correct date of birth. If your name has changed or appears differently across records, explain the discrepancy rather than editing a document to make it match.

Ask what supporting evidence is accepted for a name change. Do not create a second account under a different spelling to work around a verification problem.

## Address and residence

Proof of address and proof of legal residence can be separate checks. A bank statement showing an address may not demonstrate a legal right to live in that country.

Read the wording carefully. The provider may ask for an accepted recent bill, statement, official letter or different evidence specific to the requested check.

Confirm the allowed document age and address format. If you recently moved, update the account through the provider's normal process and ask which records it can accept while other documents are being updated.

Do not send immigration documents simply because an address check appeared. Share the relevant requested evidence through the verified service and clarify any ambiguity first.

## Recipient information

Collect the recipient's official account name, account number, bank name and any routing information required for the selected payout method.

An Indian bank deposit may need an IFSC. A traditional international bank route may also ask for a SWIFT/BIC and branch details. Use the receiving bank's current instructions instead of copying an unverified code from a search result.

Confirm changed recipient details by calling a known number. An email announcing a new account should not be your only verification, especially for a substantial payment.

## Source of funds

Source of funds means the origin of the particular money being sent. It is different from showing that you currently have enough money in an account.

A statement showing a balance may not explain whether the balance came from salary, savings, a sale, a gift or another source.

Wise's <a href="https://wise.com/help/articles/2976418/what-documents-might-i-need-for-a-large-transfer" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">large-transfer document guidance</a> gives examples of supporting records and explains that a bank statement alone may be insufficient. Its examples include documenting the name, amount and receipt date. Other providers set their own evidence requirements.

Prepare a clear chain from the original source to the account funding the transfer. Do not assume the provider can infer that chain from several unexplained transactions.

## Salary and savings

For employment income, a provider might request a payslip, employment record, tax document or statements showing salary credits. The exact combination depends on its request.

For accumulated savings, the question may be how the savings were built, rather than simply which account holds them today. Older statements or supporting income records can help explain the history where requested.

Consider this preparation example: you saved part of your salary each month in a separate account and now intend to send GBP 8,000 to India. Keep the salary evidence, savings statements and record of the movement into the funding account together.

Write a short factual timeline for yourself. It can help you answer a follow-up accurately without sending every personal financial record you possess.

## Sales and inheritance

A property sale, investment disposal or inheritance can create a large credit that looks unlike regular salary. The evidence should connect that credit to the underlying event.

For a sale, ask which agreement, completion record or broker document the provider accepts. For an inheritance, ask whether an executor's or solicitor's record and the bank credit are needed.

The point is consistency. Names, dates and amounts should make sense together. If the net bank credit differs from a headline sale value because of fees, a mortgage repayment or distribution between beneficiaries, be ready to explain that difference with genuine records.

Never alter the figures to make them look simpler. A clear explanation of a legitimate difference is better than an inaccurate document.

## Gifts and loans

If the money was given or lent to you, describing it as your savings may not answer the provider's question about its origin.

Explain who supplied the money and the nature of the arrangement. Ask whether a gift declaration, loan agreement or other supporting evidence is required.

Some reviews may also ask about the giver's source of money. Do not send another person's confidential papers without their involvement and a verified request.

Payment verification is separate from tax treatment. Acceptance by a transfer company does not establish that a gift or loan has no tax consequences in either country.

For a substantial or unusual arrangement, obtain appropriate professional advice about the underlying transaction rather than treating transfer approval as the final answer.

## Payment purpose

The provider may ask why the money is being sent. Family support, education, medical bills, a business payment and an investment are not interchangeable descriptions.

Choose the accurate purpose. If supporting evidence is requested, it could involve the relevant invoice, admission record, bill or other document suited to that purpose.

Do not choose an inaccurate category because it seems quicker. A mismatch between the stated purpose and the papers can create further questions.

For non-resident recipients, confirm the receiving account is appropriate. The Indian bank should confirm current account and transaction requirements.

## Proof of payment

Proof of payment shows that you sent money from a bank account to the transfer provider. It does not necessarily prove how you originally earned or received that money.

If funding has not been matched, the provider might ask for a payment confirmation showing the sender, destination, amount, currency, date and reference. Wise's proof-of-payment guidance illustrates this separate type of evidence.

Keep the exact reference used when funding the transfer. A missing or incorrect reference can make matching more difficult.

For a joint account, ask what evidence is needed to establish that you are an account holder. Do not assume a payment from someone else's account is permitted just because the beneficiary details are correct.

## Upload securely

Open the provider's official app or type its known website address yourself. Confirm that the document request appears inside your account or verify it with support using independently obtained contact details.

Use the secure upload route supplied by the genuine service. Do not publish passport scans, statements or transfer receipts in a public forum or send them to an unsolicited social-media contact.

Read the file requirements before uploading. Providers may request complete, readable originals and may reject cropped, edited or password-protected copies. Follow the instructions for the particular document type.

Never modify evidence with image-editing or AI tools. If you are concerned about unrelated sensitive information, ask which redactions, if any, are permitted before changing a copy.

## Avoid preventable delays

Check expiry dates, legibility, missing pages and spelling before submitting. Download statements directly from the bank when that is the requested format.

Avoid submitting many different versions of the same file without explanation. Label your own records clearly so you can identify which document was sent and when.

If a review remains pending, ask whether any specific item is missing. Repeatedly starting new transfers is not a reliable way to speed up a check.

The <a href="/guides/uk-to-india-money-transfer-time/">transfer-time guide</a> explains why verification and bank processing are separate stages. Leave a buffer before a school, property or family deadline; no general article can promise a review completion time.

## Rates during review

Preparing documents does not lock an exchange rate. Read whether the provider's quote is fixed, how long it lasts and whether funding or verification must finish before a deadline.

The <a href="/gbp-to-inr/">pound to INR converter</a> is a reference tool, not an assurance that a provider will honour a displayed rate after a review.

Ask what happens if you cancel while documents are being checked, including the return timeline and any charges. The <a href="/guides/uk-to-india-money-transfer-fees/">transfer-fees guide</a> helps you separate the customer rate from visible charges.

Do not send additional money merely because a caller says it will unlock a compliance review. Verify any unexpected payment request through official support.

## Your preparation folder

Keep a small, secure folder containing the records relevant to your planned payment: accepted identity evidence, requested address information, verified recipient details and the papers explaining the money's origin.

Add the transfer quote, funding confirmation and correspondence as the transaction progresses. This is your private record, not a bundle to upload automatically.

Use a protected device and avoid shared public computers. Remove unnecessary local copies from shared download locations through your normal secure-file process after confirming that your records are retained appropriately.

If a document is unavailable, ask about accepted alternatives. Do not forge evidence or split transfers to evade checks. An honest clarification is the right next step.

## Common questions

**Is a passport always required?**

No single identity document is universally required for every provider and customer. Check the supported documents for your circumstances.

**Does a bank statement prove the source of funds?**

It can show movement or a balance, but additional evidence may be needed to explain the original source.

**Will every small transfer need new documents?**

Not necessarily. Verification status and the provider's checks affect what is requested. A small amount does not guarantee an exemption.

**Can I send documents before starting?**

Ask the provider. Some identity checks can happen separately, while transaction-specific evidence may only be reviewed after a transfer is created.

**Will complete documents guarantee approval?**

No. Accurate evidence helps the review, but the provider still decides whether the transaction meets its requirements.
