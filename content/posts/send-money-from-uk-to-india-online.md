# Send Money from UK to India Online: A Practical Guide

## Before you start

To send money from the UK to India online, you normally create an account with a bank or money-transfer service, verify your identity, enter the recipient's details, compare the complete quote and fund the payment.

The process can take only a few screens, but a careful check matters. The exchange rate, fee, payment method, recipient details and delivery route all affect the result.

Prepare your identification, UK payment method and the recipient's Indian bank details before creating the order. A missing document or incorrect account number can delay a transfer that otherwise looks simple.

## Choose a provider

Start with providers that legally offer the service to UK customers. The <a href="https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/using-payment-service-providers">Financial Conduct Authority's guidance</a> says non-bank payment service providers must be authorised or registered and can be checked on the Financial Services Register.

Find the legal company name behind the brand because the trading name may differ from the name shown on the register. Check that the permissions cover the service being offered.

Regulatory status does not prove that one quote is cheapest. It is a basic safety check before comparing price, speed and support.

## Compare the payout

Enter the same GBP amount with several suitable providers at roughly the same time. Compare the final INR amount the recipient is expected to receive.

A service can show a low visible fee while using a weaker customer exchange rate. Another can charge a clear fee and still deliver more rupees.

Use the <a href="/guides/cheapest-way-to-send-money-uk-to-india/">cheapest transfer comparison guide</a> for a repeatable method that does not assume one provider always wins.

## Read the exchange rate

GBP/INR tells you how many Indian rupees one British pound represents. If the rate rises, the same number of pounds has a higher rupee reference value. If it falls, the reference value is lower.

The customer rate can differ from a market reference because a provider applies a margin. Compare rates in the same direction and avoid mixing a buy rate with a sell rate.

Record the timestamp. A quote can expire, and a rate viewed earlier in the day may no longer be available when you pay.

## Include every fee

Check whether the transfer fee is deducted from the sending amount or added on top. This changes both the total GBP cost and the INR payout.

Funding by card can have a different price from funding by bank transfer. A sending bank, card issuer, intermediary or recipient bank can also apply a charge depending on the route.

The <a href="/guides/uk-to-india-money-transfer-fees/">UK-to-India transfer fee guide</a> explains fixed charges, percentage fees, margins and possible receiving deductions.

Do not rely on “zero commission” alone. Review the complete pounds-paid and rupees-received figures.

## Recipient details

For an Indian bank-account payout, services commonly request the recipient's full name, account number, bank name and IFSC. Requirements can vary by service and payment type.

Copy the name as held by the bank. Check every account-number digit and the eleven-character IFSC with the recipient using a trusted channel.

Some services also ask for the recipient's address, phone number, relationship to the sender or purpose of payment. Choose the accurate purpose instead of the closest convenient label.

Do not use details sent by an unexpected person claiming the recipient changed accounts. Confirm any change independently.

## NRE and NRO accounts

An Indian resident's ordinary account and a non-resident's NRE or NRO account can have different purposes and banking treatment.

Confirm with the recipient and their bank which account should receive the money. Do not assume an account is NRE merely because the holder lives outside India.

The <a href="/guides/send-money-to-nre-account-from-uk/">NRE transfer guide</a> covers account details, purpose and record keeping for that route.

## Create your account

Use the provider's official website or app. Create a strong unique password and enable multi-factor authentication when available.

Enter your legal identity details accurately. A nickname, shortened address or inconsistent date of birth can make verification harder.

Do not create multiple accounts to avoid a limit or review. That can trigger further checks and complicate support.

## Identity verification

Providers may request a passport, driving licence, proof of address, selfie or video check. Larger or unusual payments can require source-of-funds or purpose documents.

Upload documents only through the official secure account area. Check that images are clear, complete and current.

The <a href="/guides/documents-to-send-money-uk-to-india/">documents guide</a> explains common requests and how to prepare evidence without hiding or altering information.

Verification time is not always included in an advertised delivery estimate. Complete it before an urgent deadline when possible.

## Enter the amount

Most services let you specify either how many pounds to send or how many rupees the recipient should get.

Choose a fixed receiving amount for a bill or exact obligation. Choose a fixed sending amount when your GBP budget cannot be exceeded.

Check whether the fee is inside or outside the entered amount. Review the quote after changing any field because the rate or fee tier may also change.

## Choose funding

Online transfers can be funded through a UK bank payment, debit card, existing provider balance or another supported method.

Bank funding may have a lower processing cost, while a debit card can authorise quickly. Neither advantage is universal.

The <a href="/guides/bank-transfer-vs-debit-card-uk-to-india/">bank transfer versus debit card guide</a> compares cost, limits, security checks, failed payments and refunds.

Use a payment method in your own name unless the provider explicitly supports something else.

## Bank funding steps

The provider may show a UK sort code, account number and unique reference after you create the order. Send the exact amount from your online banking.

Copy the reference exactly so incoming money can be matched. Check the payee details inside the provider account rather than from an unsolicited email.

After paying, return to the provider activity screen. Some services ask you to confirm that the bank payment was sent.

Keep the bank receipt until the recipient confirms the INR credit.

## Card funding steps

For a debit-card payment, enter the card details on the official checkout and complete the bank's security challenge.

Review the final card fee before approval. Your bank can decline the transaction because of a purchase limit, fraud control or online-payment setting.

If a card attempt remains pending, check the provider order before paying again. Repeated attempts can result in temporary authorisations or a duplicate payment.

Never share a card PIN or one-time authentication code with someone who contacts you.

## Review before paying

Confirm the sender name, recipient name, account number, IFSC, purpose, GBP cost, exchange rate, fee, INR payout and delivery estimate.

Read whether the rate is guaranteed and how long the quote remains valid. Note what happens if funding arrives after the deadline.

Take a screenshot or save the quote if the provider allows it. The completed receipt should later confirm the actual rate and payout.

## Delivery time

An online transfer can be quick, but “instant” funding does not guarantee instant recipient credit. Verification, conversion, payout processing and recipient-bank systems are separate stages.

Weekends, UK or Indian bank holidays, incorrect details and manual review can extend the estimate.

Read the <a href="/guides/uk-to-india-money-transfer-time/">transfer time guide</a> before sending money for a fixed deadline.

Pay early enough to handle a document request or rejected recipient detail.

## Track the transfer

Use the provider's activity page to follow statuses such as awaiting payment, money received, verification, converting, sent and completed.

Save the order number and any Indian payout reference. If the provider marks the transfer complete but the recipient cannot see it, ask for the bank reference.

Our <a href="/guides/track-money-transfer-uk-to-india/">tracking guide</a> explains common statuses, SWIFT references and safe escalation.

Do not publish a transaction reference or bank receipt on social media.

## If it is delayed

First check whether the provider is waiting for funding, documents or corrected recipient information. Read the newest message in the official account.

Contact support with the order reference, date, GBP amount, INR amount and concise issue description. Never provide passwords or one-time codes.

Avoid creating a second transfer until the first payment's position is clear. A duplicate can become two completed payments.

Keep support case numbers if the problem needs escalation.

## If details are wrong

Contact the provider immediately. An awaiting or processing transfer may be editable or cancellable, but a completed payout may not be recoverable.

Do not assume a bank will automatically correct a wrong account number or IFSC. Recovery depends on the payment state and recipient account.

For a suspected scam, contact your bank and provider promptly through official channels.

## Keep records

Save the initial quote, funding receipt, final conversion receipt, recipient details, transfer reference and any supporting purpose documents.

Records help with provider support, bank tracing, source-of-funds questions and personal accounting.

For regular family support, keep a simple dated list of transfers and final INR amounts. This makes future cost comparisons more reliable.

## Final checklist

Check the provider's regulatory identity and official domain. Compare final INR payouts for the same amount and funding method.

Verify the recipient name, account number, IFSC and payment purpose. Complete identity checks through the secure account.

Review the rate, every fee, funding deadline and delivery estimate before paying. Save all references and track the order until the recipient confirms credit.

## Common questions

**What details do I need to send money online to India?**

Common details include the recipient's name, account number, IFSC and payment purpose. The provider may request more information.

**Is an online transfer always cheaper than a bank?**

No. Compare the final INR payout and total GBP cost for your exact amount and payment method.

**Can I send directly to an Indian bank account?**

Many services support INR bank deposits, but availability and account requirements vary by provider.

**How long does an online UK-to-India transfer take?**

It depends on funding, verification, conversion, payout route, bank processing and holidays. Use the provider's current estimate.

**How do I know the provider is legitimate?**

Check the legal company and relevant permissions on the FCA Financial Services Register, then access the service through its official domain.
