# How Much Money Can You Send from UK to India?

## The short answer

There is no single number that answers **how much money can you send from UK to India** in every situation. Your practical limit can depend on the transfer provider, the way you fund the payment, the Indian payout route, the purpose of the transfer and the checks required for that transaction.

A provider may publish a per-transfer, daily or account limit. Your UK bank can apply its own online-payment limit. An Indian remittance route may also have separate conditions. A limit shown in an app is therefore not necessarily a universal UK or Indian legal ceiling.

Large legitimate transfers are possible through suitable regulated routes, but they commonly receive more scrutiny. Be ready to explain where the money came from, why it is being sent and who will receive it. Approval is never guaranteed merely because the amount sits below a displayed limit.

This guide provides general information, not legal, tax or financial advice. For a property purchase, business payment, investment, inheritance or other high-value transaction, confirm the route with the provider, receiving bank and an appropriately qualified adviser.

## Why limits differ

The word "limit" can describe several different things. A provider limit is the maximum its system accepts. A bank limit controls how much you can send from your account. A payout-scheme limit belongs to the route used in India. A compliance review is not a published limit at all, but it can pause a transfer while evidence is checked.

These layers can overlap. Suppose a provider allows a large international transfer but your UK bank permits a smaller online payment. You may need to arrange a bank transfer through a different channel. That does not mean dividing the transaction into artificial pieces to avoid checks.

The receiving bank may ask about the purpose or beneficiary. A family-maintenance payment is not the same as a property payment, investment or commercial invoice. Selecting an inaccurate purpose simply to make a transfer go through can create delays and record-keeping problems.

Ask a precise question: "What is the maximum for this funding method, recipient account, payout route and transfer purpose?" That is more useful than asking only for the provider's largest advertised number.

## The transfer route

India has more than one route for receiving money from abroad. A bank-to-bank transfer and a cash-oriented personal-remittance service do not necessarily use the same framework.

The Reserve Bank of India explains that its Money Transfer Service Scheme, or MTSS, is for inward personal remittances such as family maintenance and remittances for foreign tourists. Its published FAQ describes an individual remittance cap and an annual count for that particular scheme. It also says certain purposes, including property purchases, investments and credits to NRE accounts, are not allowed through MTSS.

Those MTSS conditions should not be copied across to every bank deposit. The RBI's <a href="https://www.rbi.org.in/scriptS/FAQView.aspx?Id=112" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">inward-remittance FAQ</a> distinguishes MTSS from other arrangements. A large account-to-account remittance may use a different banking route with different conditions.

This distinction explains why two apparently conflicting limit figures can both be genuine. They may describe different services. Before relying on a number, identify the route to which it belongs.

## Provider limits

Transfer companies set operational and risk limits based on account verification, currency, payment method and destination. A new or partially verified account may have a lower usable limit than a fully reviewed account.

Some providers display the maximum after you enter the currencies and funding method. Others ask customers to contact a specialist team for large payments. Published limits can change, so check the provider's current terms rather than relying on an old comparison article or screenshot.

Also distinguish the sending limit from the guaranteed conversion amount. The exchange rate may remain available only for a stated period. If your bank payment or compliance review arrives late, the quote may expire even though the transfer itself remains within the provider's limit.

## Your UK bank

Your bank may restrict online, mobile or telephone payments. The figure can differ by channel and can be lower for a new payee. Fraud-prevention checks may hold an unusual or high-value payment while the bank confirms that you authorised it.

Ask whether the cap is per transaction or per day, and whether it applies to Faster Payments, CHAPS, card funding or an international transfer. Also ask about charges. A higher-limit route can carry a different fee.

Do not assume that a declined payment means the transfer provider rejected you. The sending bank, card issuer and provider are separate parties. The error message or support team should identify which stage stopped the payment.

## Verification checks

A provider must understand its customer and monitor transactions. The amount is only one factor. A payment can be reviewed because it differs from normal account activity, has an unclear purpose, involves inconsistent names or needs additional source-of-funds evidence.

For a substantial transfer, prepare the evidence before a deadline becomes urgent. Depending on the source, that might include recent bank records, salary evidence, a completion statement, probate records, investment-sale evidence or business documents.

The evidence should form a coherent trail. A bank statement showing a balance may not explain how the balance arose. If money moved between your own accounts, keep records that connect the original source to the account used for funding.

Read the <a href="/guides/documents-to-send-money-uk-to-india/">transfer documents guide</a> for a practical preparation list. Upload only what the provider requests through its official secure channel.

## Recipient details

A large transfer is more likely to fail when recipient details are incomplete or inconsistent. Confirm the beneficiary's full account name, account number, IFSC code, bank and branch details. For a SWIFT route, verify any requested BIC or intermediary information.

Check whether the recipient account can accept the stated purpose and currency. If you are sending to your own NRE account, use a route that supports that credit. The <a href="/guides/send-money-to-nre-account-from-uk/">NRE account transfer guide</a> explains the practical distinction.

Do not reuse old details without asking the recipient to confirm them. Bank mergers, branch changes and account restrictions can affect a transfer. Independently verify any last-minute change received by email or messaging app because changed payment details are a common fraud warning.

## Purpose matters

The amount alone does not decide whether a transfer is appropriate. The reason for the payment affects the route, documents and possible tax treatment.

Family support, your own savings, a gift, tuition, property, an investment and payment for services are different purposes. A provider may request an invoice, admission record, sale agreement, gift explanation or proof of relationship.

Do not label a commercial payment as family maintenance. Do not label a loan as a gift. A truthful description protects the records of both sender and recipient and helps the receiving bank apply the correct reporting category.

If the money is related to a property transaction, company, trust or estate, obtain specialist guidance before sending. The transfer provider processes money; it does not necessarily decide your legal or tax position.

## Transfer tax

A transfer limit and a tax rule answer different questions. Moving money does not automatically make it taxable, but the underlying receipt or transaction may have tax consequences.

For example, money returned to your own account is different from salary, business income, sale proceeds, a loan or a gift. A gift from a defined relative may be treated differently from money received from another person under Indian tax rules.

Keep evidence of the source, purpose and relationship. The separate <a href="/guides/is-money-sent-from-uk-to-india-taxable/">UK-to-India transfer tax guide</a> explains the main questions without treating every remittance as income.

For a significant payment, ask an Indian tax professional about the recipient's circumstances and a UK adviser about any issue connected with the sender or source. A provider saying "transfer permitted" is not a tax ruling.

First, confirm that the provider supports the account type and purpose. Second, check its current per-transfer and account limits. Third, check your bank's funding limit and charge. ## Avoid splitting

Do not divide a large transfer into smaller payments for the purpose of avoiding verification or reporting checks. Related transactions can be considered together, and artificial splitting may create more questions rather than fewer.

There are legitimate reasons to send in stages, such as contract milestones or rate-risk management. Document that reason and ask the provider how repeated payments affect limits and fees.

Multiple small transfers can also cost more if each carries a fixed fee. Compare the total recipient amount across the complete plan, not the attractive appearance of one small quote.

## Before sending

Confirm the exact route, current provider limit, funding limit and recipient-account eligibility. Check the purpose description and gather source-of-funds evidence.

Compare the exchange-rate margin, visible fees and possible receiving deductions. Read the quote-expiry terms and cancellation process. Verify recipient details independently, especially after any unexpected change.

Keep the quote, transfer reference, funding confirmation and recipient receipt. These records can help with tracing, tax questions or a later refund.

## Common questions

**Is there one UK legal limit for sending money to India?**

There is no single practical number that covers every provider and route. Current provider, bank, payout-scheme and compliance conditions must be checked for the specific transfer.

**Can I send a very large amount in one transfer?**

Possibly, through a suitable route and provider. Expect additional evidence and confirm the receiving purpose and account before funding it.

**Does a transfer below the provider limit avoid checks?**

No. Checks can depend on risk, account activity, identity, source, recipient and purpose as well as amount.

**Should I split a transfer to stay below a limit?**

Do not split payments to avoid controls. If staged payments have a genuine commercial or practical reason, document it and ask how the provider handles them.

**Is the maximum amount also the cheapest amount to send?**

No. Limits and value are separate. Compare the complete INR payout, fees, exchange-rate margin, speed and route for the amount you actually need to send.
