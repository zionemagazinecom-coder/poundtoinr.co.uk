# How to Track a Money Transfer from UK to India

## Start with the reference

To track a money transfer from the UK to India, first find the provider's order number or transfer reference. It is usually shown in the confirmation email, receipt or activity screen.

Keep this reference private. Share it only with the transfer provider, sending bank or intended recipient when necessary.

The reference identifies the order, but it does not always identify every bank-to-bank movement. A SWIFT payment can also have a bank message reference or UETR.

## Check the activity page

The provider's official app or website is normally the best first source. Open the exact transfer and read the current status, estimated arrival and any action request.

Avoid relying only on an email notification because the activity page may be newer. Refresh reasonably rather than creating another payment.

If the status asks for documents or recipient details, use the secure account area instead of replying to an unexpected message with sensitive files.

## Created

“Created”, “set up” or “awaiting payment” usually means the order exists but the provider has not confirmed funding.

For bank funding, check that you sent the correct amount to the displayed account and used the required reference. A missing reference can delay matching.

For card funding, confirm whether the bank approved the payment and whether the provider shows it as completed rather than pending.

## Money received

This status normally means the provider has received or matched the pounds. It does not necessarily mean the recipient has rupees.

Conversion, compliance checks and payout processing may still follow. Read the estimated delivery time attached to the transaction.

## Verification in progress

A transfer can pause while the service verifies identity, source of funds, purpose or recipient details.

The request may be routine, especially for a new customer, changed behaviour or larger amount. Respond with clear, complete documents through the official channel.

Do not edit a document to hide information. If a request is unclear, ask support what document type and date range are required.

Our <a href="/guides/documents-to-send-money-uk-to-india/">transfer documents guide</a> covers common evidence and safe preparation.

## Converting

“Converting” means the provider is exchanging GBP into INR or preparing that step. The rate may already be locked, or it may depend on when funding arrived.

Check the receipt for the final exchange rate, fee and payout. A reference calculator cannot replace the completed transaction record.

If a guaranteed rate expired before funding, the provider may have issued a revised quote. Review it before assuming an error occurred.

## Sent to the recipient

“Sent”, “paid out” or “on its way” means the provider has initiated the Indian payout. The recipient's bank may still need to post the credit.

Ask the recipient to check the correct account, recent transactions and any bank message. The account balance notification can lag behind the transaction list.

Do not ask the recipient to share passwords, PINs or one-time codes. Those are never needed to receive a legitimate bank credit.

## Completed

“Completed” generally means the provider considers its payout finished. Save the completion receipt and compare its recipient details with the intended account.

If the recipient cannot see the money, ask them to contact their bank with the available payout reference, amount, date and sender information.

Contact the provider as well. It can confirm whether the payment was returned, rejected or successfully credited according to its records.

## SWIFT tracking

A traditional international bank transfer may travel through SWIFT messaging and intermediary banks. The sending bank can provide a payment confirmation, often called an MT103 for relevant customer transfers.

Modern SWIFT payments may include a Unique End-to-end Transaction Reference. The <a href="https://www.swift.com/your-needs/banking/what-unique-end-to-end-transaction-reference-uetr">linked SWIFT explanation</a> describes the UETR as a 36-character reference used to identify a payment across its lifecycle.

Customers do not necessarily receive direct access to bank tracking systems. Ask the sending bank to trace the transfer using the appropriate reference.

Read the <a href="/guides/swift-transfer-from-uk-to-india/">SWIFT transfer guide</a> for correspondent-bank timing and fee considerations.

## Local payout tracking

Some transfer services collect GBP locally and arrange an INR payout through an Indian banking partner. The provider reference may therefore be more useful than a SWIFT reference.

Ask support for the local payout or bank reference when the status says completed but the credit is missing.

The recipient bank can search more effectively when given the exact INR amount, value date and transaction reference.

## Bank-funded orders

For a bank-funded order, keep both sides of the trail: the UK bank receipt and the transfer-provider order.

The bank receipt should show amount, date, recipient account details and payment reference. The provider screen should show whether those funds were matched.

## NRE account transfers

If the recipient is an NRE account, verify that the account is permitted to receive the intended inward remittance and that the details match the account holder.

A bank may request remittance purpose information or issue an advice after credit. Keep the transfer receipt for future tax, banking or source-of-funds questions.

## Common delay reasons

Delays can come from unmatched funding, weekends, bank holidays, compliance review, recipient-detail errors, intermediary processing or technical issues.

A changed delivery estimate does not tell you which cause applies. Read any status message and contact support with the reference.

Avoid sending a duplicate while the first transfer remains unresolved. Two successful payments can be harder to recover than one delayed payment.

## Recipient detail errors

An incorrect account number or IFSC can lead to rejection, return or, in some circumstances, credit to an unintended valid account.

Contact the provider immediately when you notice an error. A completed transfer may not be cancellable, and recovery is not guaranteed.

Confirm recipient details independently before every first payment and after any account change.

## Returned payments

A transfer can be returned when the recipient account is closed, details are invalid, the bank rejects the purpose or required information is missing.

The amount returned may differ if bank charges or currency conversion apply. Ask for a written breakdown rather than guessing from the balance.

Refund timing depends on the route. Save return references and compare the credited GBP with the original payment.

## When to contact support

Contact the provider when the estimated arrival has passed, a status has not changed for an unusual period, or the recipient bank cannot locate a completed payout.

Provide the order reference, transfer date, GBP amount, INR amount, recipient name and a concise description. Do not send passwords or full card credentials.

Ask a specific question: whether funding was matched, whether verification is pending, or which payout reference the recipient bank should trace.

## Escalation

If normal support cannot resolve the issue, use the provider's formal complaint process. Keep dates, case numbers and copies of relevant messages.

For a bank transfer, ask the bank whether it can initiate a trace or recall. A recall is a request, not a guarantee that money will return.

Use only official contact details. Search-result adverts and unsolicited “recovery” agents can impersonate financial services.

## Track safely

Never publish a transfer reference on social media. Do not share a bank statement without checking what information is visible.

Provider staff should not need your online-banking password, card PIN or one-time authentication code.

If someone asks for another payment to “release” a personal transfer, verify the request through the provider's official support channel before doing anything.

## Keep useful records

Save the quote, funding receipt, conversion receipt, provider reference, payout reference and messages about delays or returns.

For larger transfers, keep evidence of source and purpose. The <a href="/guides/how-much-money-can-you-send-uk-to-india/">transfer limits guide</a> explains why amount and customer circumstances can affect checks.

Good records make tracking easier and support later bank, tax or complaint questions.

## Final checklist

Open the official activity page and identify the current status. Confirm whether the provider is awaiting funding, reviewing documents, converting or waiting for recipient-bank credit.

Collect the right reference for the payment route. Ask the recipient to check the correct account and transaction list.

If the estimate has passed, contact support once with complete details. Keep the case number and avoid a duplicate transfer.

## Common questions

**Where is my UK-to-India transfer reference?**

It is usually in the provider's activity page, confirmation email or receipt. Bank and SWIFT routes may have additional references.

**Does completed mean the recipient has the money?**

It normally means the provider finished its payout, but the recipient bank's display can lag. Use the payout reference to investigate.

**What is a UETR?**

It is a unique 36-character reference used to identify a SWIFT payment through its lifecycle.

**Should I resend a delayed transfer?**

Not until the first payment's status is clear. A duplicate can result in two successful transfers.

**What should I give support?**

Provide the transfer reference, dates, amounts, recipient name and issue summary, but never passwords, PINs or one-time codes.
