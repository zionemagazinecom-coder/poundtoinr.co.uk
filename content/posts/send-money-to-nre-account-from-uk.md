# How to Send Money to an NRE Account from UK

## Start with the account

To **send money to an NRE account from UK**, first confirm that the beneficiary account is genuinely an NRE account and that the account holder is eligible to operate it. Then compare a complete GBP-to-INR quote, enter the bank details carefully and provide the correct transfer purpose.

NRE stands for Non-Resident External. It is an Indian rupee account designed for eligible non-resident customers and can receive permitted funds remitted from outside India.

The Reserve Bank of India's January 2025 account guidance states that NRE accounts can be opened by NRIs and PIOs, which includes eligible OCI cardholders resident outside India. Individual situations can differ, so the account holder should confirm current status with the bank.

This guide explains the transfer process but is not personal tax, legal or banking advice.

## Check eligibility

The account holder's residential status matters. An NRE account is not simply a normal resident savings account with another label.

The RBI defines an NRI as an Indian citizen resident outside India. Its guidance also describes eligible persons of Indian origin and OCI cardholders for account purposes.

The broader background to <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-resident_Indian_and_Overseas_Citizen_of_India" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">non-resident Indian and OCI status</a> can help explain the terminology, but the bank and current regulation determine account eligibility.

If the account holder has returned to India or their residential status has changed, they should ask the bank whether the account must be redesignated. Do not assume an old NRE account status remains correct indefinitely.

## NRE and NRO difference

NRE and NRO accounts serve different purposes. The correct choice depends on the source of money and how the account will be used.

NRE accounts are commonly used for permitted overseas earnings remitted to India. RBI guidance describes NRE balances as repatriable, subject to applicable rules and banking checks.

NRO accounts are generally used for legitimate rupee transactions and Indian-source income such as rent, pension or dividends. Repatriation treatment is different and subject to conditions.

Do not select an account type because one sounds more convenient. The recipient should confirm the suitable account with their authorised bank.

## Confirm bank details

Ask the recipient for details directly from a recent bank statement, official banking app or bank-issued account confirmation.

Check the account holder's full name, account number, account type, bank name, branch and Indian bank code. The provider may request additional routing information.

Names should match bank records. Initials, spacing or an outdated surname can trigger a review when the transfer details do not align.

Do not rely on bank details copied from an old chat without reconfirming them. Fraudsters sometimes impersonate recipients and provide replacement account information.

For a new or changed beneficiary, verify details through a separate trusted communication method.

## Choose the purpose

The transfer provider may ask why the money is being sent. Choose the answer that accurately reflects the payment.

Examples can include transferring personal overseas earnings, family maintenance or savings. Available labels vary by provider and banking route.

Property, investment, trade and charitable payments can involve different documentation or permitted channels. Do not select a personal-transfer purpose for a different transaction simply to proceed faster.

If no option fits, ask the provider or receiving bank before confirming. An accurate purpose supports compliance and reduces the risk of delay.

## Compare complete quotes

Request quotes for the same GBP amount, payment method and delivery speed. Obtain them within a short period because the market can move.

Record the customer rate, visible fee, total pounds charged, guaranteed INR payout and expected arrival date.

A provider advertising zero fee may still include a margin inside the exchange rate. Another provider can charge a fee but deliver more rupees.

Choose the service that supports NRE account transfers and clearly identifies the amount expected to reach the account.

## Understand the margin

Suppose the reference rate is 129.5956 and the provider offers 127.90. The gap is 1.6956 rupees per pound.

For GBP 1,000, the reference result is INR 129,595.60. At 127.90, the converted amount is INR 127,900 before a separate fee.

The difference is INR 1,695.60. That amount demonstrates why a small-looking rate gap matters on a larger transfer.

## Review transfer fees

Costs can include a visible provider fee, rate margin, card charge, intermediary fee or receiving-bank deduction.

Check whether the provider guarantees the final INR amount after all known charges. Traditional bank wires may involve different routing from transfer services using local payout arrangements.

For a fixed recipient target, compare the total pounds required. For a fixed send budget, compare the final rupees delivered.

## Select the funding method

Bank payment is often suitable for planned transfers, while card funding can be faster. Pricing and limits vary.

A provider may charge extra for card funding. Your card issuer could also apply a separate charge that does not appear in the provider quote.

Check when the rate becomes locked. If it is guaranteed only after the provider receives funds, a delayed bank payment can lead to repricing.

Use an account in the sender's name when required by the provider. Third-party funding can trigger additional checks or rejection.

## Prepare documents

Providers must carry out identity and anti-money-laundering checks. Larger or unusual transfers may require evidence of the source and purpose of funds.

Prepare a passport or other accepted identity document, proof of address and bank statements when requested.

Source-of-funds evidence may include salary records, savings history, a property-sale document, inheritance paperwork or a business record appropriate to the transaction.

Provide genuine, complete documents. Cropped screenshots or unexplained large credits can lead to follow-up questions.

Start the verification process before a time-sensitive payment. A strong exchange-rate quote is not useful if compliance is incomplete when the guarantee expires.

## Check limits

Transfer providers can apply daily, monthly or transaction limits. Banks may also impose online-payment limits.

The receiving account can have operational conditions set by the bank. Confirm them before sending an unusually large amount.

Do not split transfers to evade legitimate reporting, verification or provider limits. Splitting is appropriate only as a genuine timing or budgeting decision with clear records.

Ask the provider about large-transfer support when the amount exceeds its normal online range.

## Review the guarantee

A rate guarantee states how long a quote remains valid and what must happen before expiry.

The provider may need to receive your pounds before the guarantee deadline. Creating an order alone may not be sufficient.

Read what happens if funding arrives late. The transfer could be cancelled or recalculated at a new rate.

Save the confirmation showing the rate, fee, payout, transfer reference and estimated delivery date.

## Send a test payment

For a new beneficiary or provider, a small test payment can confirm that the details and delivery route work.

A test creates an extra fee in some cases, so weigh the cost against the size and importance of the main transfer.

Wait for the recipient to confirm the credit and account description. Then recheck the beneficiary details before sending the remainder.

Do not assume a successful payment to one Indian account proves that another account type will work identically.

## Track the transfer

Keep the provider's tracking number and confirmation. Share only the information the recipient needs to identify the credit.

Statuses such as funded, converted, sent and delivered can describe different stages. A delivered status may mean the provider released funds to the banking system, not necessarily that the recipient has checked the account.

If the transfer is delayed, contact the provider with the reference number. The receiving bank may also need trace information.

Avoid sending a duplicate payment until the first transfer's status is clear.

## Verify the credit

Ask the recipient to confirm the exact INR amount credited, date and any bank description or deduction.

Compare that amount with the guaranteed payout. If there is a difference, review whether the quote allowed receiving or intermediary charges.

Keep records of the pounds paid, customer rate, fee and INR received. These records are useful for repeated transfers and personal accounting.

The bank can explain how the credit appears in the NRE account and whether additional information is required.

## Repatriation and tax

NRE balances are generally described as repatriable under RBI rules, but account use and tax treatment depend on current law and personal circumstances.

Interest treatment, residency and reporting can involve rules in both India and the United Kingdom.

Do not treat a transfer provider's checkout message as personal tax advice. Use a qualified adviser for material cross-border tax questions.

The account holder should keep residency and bank records current. A change in status can affect how the account should be maintained.

## Transfer timing

There is no guaranteed best day to convert pounds to rupees. GBP/INR can move after UK or Indian economic news.

The <a href="/guides/best-time-to-send-money-from-uk-to-india/">transfer timing guide</a> explains how to set a target and respect a delivery deadline.

For essential payments, allow time for funding, verification and receiving-bank processing. Waiting for a forecast can create unnecessary deadline risk.

Weekends and holidays may affect processing even when a provider accepts the order online.

## Security checks

Verify the provider's regulatory status and legal identity through official sources. Do not rely only on a badge displayed on its website.

Use strong authentication and access the provider through a saved official address. Be cautious with links sent in unexpected messages.

Never share a banking password, one-time code or remote device access. A legitimate transfer does not require those credentials.

Confirm any request to change beneficiary details directly with the account holder through a trusted channel.

## Common mistakes

The first mistake is confusing an NRE account with an NRO or resident account.

The second is entering beneficiary information from an old unverified message.

The third is choosing an inaccurate transfer purpose.

The fourth is comparing only visible fees and ignoring the exchange-rate margin.

The fifth is starting verification after a short rate guarantee has already begun.

The sixth is sending a duplicate payment when the first transfer is merely delayed.

## Can anyone open an NRE account?

No. NRE accounts are intended for eligible non-resident customers under RBI rules. The bank must verify eligibility and documentation.

The account holder should ask the authorised bank about their circumstances.

## Can UK salary be sent to NRE?

Permitted overseas earnings are commonly remitted to NRE accounts, subject to bank and regulatory checks.

Use accurate source information and retain salary or bank records if the provider asks for evidence.

## Is the live rate guaranteed?

No. The live page provides a reference benchmark. The provider's final confirmation determines the customer rate and guaranteed payout.

Check the quote expiry and funding conditions before paying.
