# SWIFT Transfer from UK to India: Fees, Time and Steps

## Start here

A **SWIFT transfer from UK to India** is an international bank payment in which financial institutions exchange standardised payment instructions over the SWIFT network. The network carries secure messages; banks and other financial institutions handle the actual movement and settlement of funds.

That distinction matters. SWIFT is not the bank holding your money, setting your GBP/INR rate or deciding every fee. The sending bank, any intermediary institutions and the receiving Indian bank determine the practical route.

Before sending, check the complete cost, recipient details, expected delivery time and how a failed or delayed payment will be traced.

This guide is general information, not personal financial, legal or tax advice.

## What SWIFT does

SWIFT provides a common, secure messaging system used by banks and financial institutions for cross-border transaction instructions. Its official <a href="https://www.swift.com/about-us/who-we-are/what-swift" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">explanation of the SWIFT network</a> describes how a customer's bank sends a message to the recipient bank for an international payment.

The message identifies the financial institutions, beneficiary and payment instructions in a structured format. It does not mean the money physically travels inside the message.

Banks settle the value through accounts they hold with one another or through correspondent relationships. If the sending and receiving banks do not have a direct relationship, an intermediary may help complete the route.

A technically successful message does not by itself prove the recipient has received usable funds. Settlement, compliance review and posting to the beneficiary account must also finish.

## SWIFT and BIC

People often use SWIFT code and BIC as interchangeable terms. A Bank Identifier Code identifies a particular financial institution and can include branch information.

An eight-character BIC normally identifies the institution's head office or primary location. An eleven-character code can include a three-character branch identifier.

Do not guess the code from a bank name. Ask the recipient for details from an official bank statement, secure banking app or bank confirmation.

Some Indian banks publish different SWIFT details for particular branches or route incoming payments through a central foreign-exchange branch. Follow current instructions from the receiving bank.

The Indian Financial System Code is important for domestic routing, but it is not a replacement for a SWIFT/BIC when the international payment form specifically requires one.

## Payment route

A direct route is possible when the UK sending bank and Indian receiving bank have an appropriate account relationship.

Otherwise, one or more correspondent banks may sit between them. A correspondent holds accounts or provides services that allow another institution to send or receive value in a currency or market where it lacks direct access.

Each additional institution can add processing time, compliance checks and possible deductions. The route may not be fully visible when you first create the payment.

Ask the sending bank whether it can estimate intermediary charges and whether the beneficiary will receive a guaranteed amount.

## Recipient details

Accurate beneficiary information is essential because international recalls and amendments can be slow and may cost money.

You will usually need the recipient's full account name, address where requested, account number, bank name, branch address and SWIFT/BIC. The bank may also ask for the Indian routing code, recipient phone number or transfer purpose.

Enter the account name exactly as held by the bank. Initials, spelling changes or a business trading name that differs from the legal account can trigger review.

Confirm the details through a trusted channel, especially if they arrived by email or changed recently. Fraudsters sometimes imitate a known recipient and substitute their own account.

Never send a second payment to correct the first until the bank confirms the original status.

## Transfer purpose

The sending or receiving bank may request a purpose because cross-border payments are subject to regulatory, tax and compliance requirements.

Choose the description that truthfully matches the payment, such as family maintenance, education, medical costs or another legitimate purpose.

Business invoices, investments, property transactions and gifts can require different documentation. Do not choose an inaccurate category merely because it looks easier.

Keep supporting records, including invoices, admission letters, medical documents, employment evidence or source-of-funds statements where relevant.

If the recipient uses an NRE or NRO account, confirm that the payment purpose and source are suitable for that account before sending.

## NRE and NRO accounts

An NRE account generally receives permitted overseas income and has a different repatriation and tax treatment from an NRO account. An NRO account is commonly used for legitimate income arising in India and other permitted rupee transactions.

The correct account depends on the recipient's status, source of funds and reason for payment. Do not infer it from an old account number.

Current requirements should be confirmed with the receiving Indian bank or a qualified adviser.

## GBP or INR

The payment form may allow you to send pounds, rupees or another settlement currency. The choice affects where conversion occurs and which institution sets the exchange rate.

If the UK bank converts GBP to INR before sending, review its customer rate and fee. If GBP is sent onward, a correspondent or receiving bank may perform the conversion later.

Ask who controls the conversion and whether the final INR amount is known before confirmation.

## Transfer fees

A SWIFT payment can involve more than one charge.

The sending bank may charge an international-payment fee. A correspondent may deduct a handling charge, and the receiving bank may apply an incoming-payment or conversion fee.

Forms sometimes describe fee arrangements as OUR, SHA or BEN. In broad terms, OUR asks the sender to bear charges, SHA divides charges and BEN allows charges to be borne by the beneficiary. The practical outcome depends on the banks and route, so read the bank's exact terms rather than relying only on the label.

Even an OUR instruction may not create an absolute payout guarantee in every route. Ask the sending bank what it actually covers.

## Example costs

Consider a GBP 2,000 payment. At the checked reference rate, the benchmark is INR 261,213.40.

If the bank offers 128.90 and converts the full amount, the starting INR value is INR 257,800. If it also charges GBP 15 on top, the sender pays GBP 2,015.

If GBP 15 is deducted before conversion instead, only GBP 1,985 is converted. At 128.90, that produces INR 255,866.50 before possible correspondent deductions.

The two fee treatments are materially different. Ask whether each charge is added to the debit or taken from the payment.

For an intermediary deduction of INR 500, the final credit in the second example would fall to INR 255,366.50. The receiving statement gives the definitive result.

## How long it takes

A SWIFT transfer from the UK to India commonly takes more than the few seconds required to send a message. The payment must pass through settlement, screening and account-posting stages.

Delivery can range from the same working day to several business days. The exact time depends on submission cut-off, currency, route, time zones, weekends, holidays and compliance review.

A bank may advertise a typical time rather than a guarantee. A message can reach the next institution quickly while the funds wait for processing.

The <a href="/guides/uk-to-india-money-transfer-time/">UK to India transfer time guide</a> explains cut-offs, funding stages and common delays in more detail.

Send early when the money is needed for a fixed deadline.

## Common delays

Incorrect or incomplete beneficiary details are a frequent source of delay. A mismatch can lead to an investigation, repair request or return.

Compliance screening may pause a payment when the amount, recipient or purpose needs additional evidence. This does not automatically mean wrongdoing; financial institutions must review certain transactions.

An intermediary can also request more information. Weekend and public-holiday schedules in either country may hold processing until the next working day.

Currency conversion can miss a cut-off even when the payment instruction was submitted on time.

Respond promptly to genuine requests through the bank's official secure channel. Do not send identity documents to an unverified email address.

## Tracking the transfer

Save the payment confirmation, debit date, amount, currency and bank reference.

Many international payment records include a transaction reference that the sending bank can use to trace the route. Some banks provide progress information inside online banking, while others require a support request.

If the recipient has not received the money after the expected window, first confirm that the sending account was debited and that no bank message is waiting for you.

Ask the sending bank for a trace and the relevant payment reference. The recipient can give that reference to the Indian bank's inward-remittance team.

Do not publish a full payment message online because it can contain personal and banking information.

## Returns and recalls

A bank can return a payment when the account is closed, details are invalid, the purpose is unsupported or compliance requirements are not met.

The returned amount may be lower than the original debit because exchange rates and bank charges can change during the process.

A recall is a request, not a guaranteed cancellation. Once the beneficiary bank has credited or released the funds, recovery can require the recipient's consent or further investigation.

Contact the sending bank immediately after discovering an error. Give clear information but do not attempt to negotiate directly with an unknown intermediary.

Keep all correspondence and complaint references until the matter is resolved.

## Provider alternative

A specialist payment provider may accept a local UK bank transfer, convert the pounds and arrange an INR payout through an Indian banking partner.

This can reduce the number of correspondent steps, but it is not automatically cheaper or faster. Compare the legal provider, protection, customer rate, fees, delivery promise and final payout.

Some provider transfers are still supported by international banking infrastructure even when the customer experience looks local.

Use the same GBP amount and funding method when comparing a provider quote with a bank SWIFT quote.

Choose the route whose complete terms suit the payment rather than assuming one technology label proves quality.

## Security checks

Use the official bank website, app or verified branch contact. Check the exact domain before signing in.

Confirm new or changed recipient details through a separate trusted channel. Invoice fraud often begins with a convincing message announcing a new account.

Never disclose passwords or one-time codes to a caller. Do not install remote-access software at someone's request to complete a transfer.

Be cautious when a person creates urgency, asks you to misstate the purpose or promises a guaranteed investment return.

If fraud is suspected, contact the sending bank immediately using the number on its official website or your card.

## Before you send

Confirm the recipient's legal account name and account number.

Verify the current SWIFT/BIC and branch instructions with the receiving bank.

Select the truthful transfer purpose and prepare supporting documents.

Check who converts GBP to INR and compare the rate with the reference.

Ask how sending, correspondent and receiving charges are allocated.

Review the expected final INR amount and delivery window.

Save the transaction receipt and tracking reference.

These checks take a few minutes and can prevent expensive repairs or returns.

## Common questions

**Does SWIFT itself move my money?**

No. SWIFT carries secure financial messages. Banks and other financial institutions handle settlement and account credits.

**How long does a UK-to-India SWIFT transfer take?**

It may arrive the same working day or take several business days, depending on the route, cut-offs, currency conversion and compliance checks.

**Can an intermediary bank deduct a fee?**

Yes. A correspondent or receiving institution may apply a charge depending on the route and fee arrangement.

**Do I need both a SWIFT code and IFSC?**

The bank may request a SWIFT/BIC for international identification and an IFSC or other information for local routing. Follow the receiving bank's current instructions.

**Can I cancel a mistaken transfer?**

Contact the sending bank immediately. A recall can be requested, but recovery is not guaranteed after the payment has progressed or been credited.

## Final view

A reliable **SWIFT transfer from UK to India** depends on accurate beneficiary details, a clear payment purpose and a complete cost comparison.

Remember that SWIFT carries the instruction while financial institutions move and settle the funds. Compare the final rupee credit, not only the transfer fee, and keep the reference needed to trace the payment.
